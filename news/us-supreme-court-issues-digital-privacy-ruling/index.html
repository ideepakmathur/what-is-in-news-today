Supreme Court Mandates Fourth Amendment Warrants for Commercial Geofence Tracking

Verified Primary Reporting: SCOTUSblog & Washington Post

The Big Picture • 3 Key Facts

Decisive 7-2 Opinion: The Supreme Court held that law enforcement and intelligence agencies cannot circumvent Fourth Amendment warrant protections by purchasing bulk smartphone location records from commercial data aggregators.
Reasonable Expectation of Privacy: Writing for the majority, the Chief Justice affirmed that continuous algorithmic tracking of citizens' digital movements invades core constitutional zones of privacy.
Closure of the Data-Broker Loophole: The landmark precedent invalidates federal, state, and municipal contracts that previously acquired geofenced location telemetry without judicial oversight.
Why It Matters

Protects 330 million Americans from warrantless algorithmic dragnet surveillance. It firmly establishes that carrying a modern smartphone does not constitute implied consent to governmental tracking of your home, place of worship, doctor visits, or private associations.

Constitutional Consensus

Civil liberties organizations and privacy advocates praised the ruling as the most critical modernization of constitutional search doctrines since Carpenter v. United States.

Dissenting View & Law Enforcement Concerns

The two dissenting justices warned that mandating formal judicial warrants for commercial datasets could slow investigative responsiveness during kidnapping, human trafficking, or immediate terror threat investigations.
