Nvidia Deploys Next-Gen Blackwell Ultra AI Superclusters as Q3 Data Center Revenue Surges to Historic $35 Billion

Verified Primary Authorities: Nvidia Press & SEC Filings • U.S. Department of Energy (DOE) • Open Compute Project (OCP)

The Big Picture • 3 Key Facts

Full-Scale Production Deployment: Nvidia initiated mass delivery of liquid-cooled GB200 NVL72 rack-scale systems across North American cloud facilities. Interconnecting 72 Blackwell GPUs and 36 Grace CPUs over NVLink 5 switches, each rack delivers 1.4 exaflops of AI inference capability.
Historic $35 Billion Data Center Revenue: Quarterly data center division revenue crossed $35.1 billion—a 112% year-over-year surge—driven by the rapid shift toward multi-step reasoning models (OpenAI o1/o3, DeepSeek) requiring massive continuous inference clusters over training-only setups.
U.S. National Laboratory Supercomputing: The U.S. Department of Energy finalized deployment partnerships integrating Blackwell clusters into Argonne and Oak Ridge National Laboratories for national biosecurity drug design, nuclear fusion plasma containment, and grid resilience modeling.
Why It Matters for American Infrastructure & Sovereignty

Semiconductor supercomputing has officially transitioned from experimental research into vital national utility infrastructure—mirroring the historical significance of the interstate highway system and electrical grid. Dense liquid cooling and 25x inference efficiency gains ensure that domestic American companies maintain decisive technological leadership while significantly moderating regional power consumption.

The Architecture Behind the Exaflop Era

Unlike preceding GPU generations sold primarily as discrete PCIe or SXM motherboards, the Blackwell GB200 is engineered exclusively as a unified rack-level computer. The GB200 NVL72 houses 18 compute trays interconnected by two miles of copper cabling and custom NVLink Spine backplanes, transferring 130 terabytes per second of aggregate bandwidth without optical transceivers.

This copper-first, direct-to-chip liquid cooling architecture reduces power overhead by 40% compared to legacy air-cooled data centers. Hyperscalers including Microsoft Azure, Google Cloud, Amazon Web Services, and Oracle Cloud Infrastructure have already integrated dedicated cooling loops to accommodate the 120-kilowatt thermal design power per rack.
