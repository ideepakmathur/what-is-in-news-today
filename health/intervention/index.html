SAMHSA Expands 988 Mobile Crisis Teams as CRAFT Intervention Surpasses Confrontational Methods

Verified Primary Authorities: SAMHSA Crisis Services Guidelines • National Alliance on Mental Illness (NAMI) • American Psychological Association

The Big Picture • 3 Key Facts

CRAFT Approach Replaces Coercion: Clinical trials confirm the Community Reinforcement and Family Training (CRAFT) model successfully engages 67% to 71% of treatment-refusing individuals into care—more than double the rate of Johnson-style surprise confrontations.
Rapid Mobile Crisis Deployment: Federal appropriations allocated $430M to pair licensed mental health clinicians with certified peer specialists for 988 emergency calls, de-escalating psychiatric crises without law enforcement involvement in 88% of calls.
Emergency Department Warm Handoffs: Hospital systems adopting 24/7 peer intervention navigators report a 44% decline in 30-day readmissions and immediate treatment linkage for substance-induced crises.
Transforming How Families Intervene

Replaces painful family ultimatums with non-judgmental positive reinforcement and natural consequences. Equips parents, spouses, and community members with evidence-backed conversational strategies that preserve relationships while opening the door to voluntary clinical care.

CRAFT Behavioral Model

Focuses on training the Concerned Significant Other (CSO) to reward sober behaviors, withdraw attention during substance use, and capitalize on moments of treatment receptivity, resulting in sustained long-term engagement.

988 De-Escalation Protocols

Mobile crisis teams respond in plain clothes with unmarked vehicles to preserve patient dignity, conduct on-site clinical risk assessments, and arrange direct voluntary admission to crisis stabilization centers.
